Dear [Sir/Madam / named officer],

I am an undergraduate student at [university / department], writing to request access to
the Department of Environment's historical air-quality monitoring data for a
non-commercial academic research project.

My thesis develops and evaluates data-driven (statistical and machine-learning) methods
for short-horizon air-quality forecasting and risk classification, with an emphasis on
rigorous validation — every result is measured against a simple no-skill baseline, and
the work is careful to report where such methods do *not* add value. A recurring
limitation I have documented is the lack of an openly accessible, multi-pollutant,
multi-year ground-truth record for Dhaka and other Bangladeshi cities: reference-grade
data is not currently available through the international open-data platforms
(for example OpenAQ) that research of this kind normally draws on.

I am aware that the Department operates a substantial national network — the National
Air Quality Management Plan 2024–2030 describes 16 continuous air monitoring stations
and 15 compact monitoring stations across 13 cities — and that the Department publishes
a real-time Air Quality Index together with daily and monthly reports. The same plan
sets out an intention to provide online access to full data archives for students,
researchers, and decision-makers (§3.3.4). In that spirit, I would be very grateful for
access to historical station data for research use.

Specifically, if it is possible, I would like to ask:

1. Whether historical (not only real-time) measurements from the CAMS and compact
   monitoring stations can be made available for academic research — ideally as a bulk
   download (CSV or similar) or through a documented data interface.
2. What the archive covers: which stations and cities, which pollutants (PM2.5, PM10,
   CO, NO2, O3, SO2), at what time resolution (e.g. hourly), and over what date range.
3. Any conditions attached to research use — attribution, a data-sharing agreement, or
   restrictions on redistribution — which I would be glad to comply with.

To be clear about intent: the data would be used solely for academic research and would
be attributed to the Department of Environment in any resulting thesis or publication. I
am not requesting it for any commercial purpose, and I am happy to sign any usage
agreement the Department requires or to complete a formal application if one exists.

If there is a more appropriate office, form, or contact for this request, I would be
grateful if you could point me to it. I am also happy to provide a short description of
the project or a letter of support from my supervisor on request.

Thank you very much for your time and for the Department's work on air-quality
monitoring in Bangladesh.

Yours faithfully,

### Notes for the sender (delete before sending)

- **Verify the recipient.** Find the current Air Quality Management Cell / CASE project
  contact on the DoE site rather than relying on a cached address. If no email is
  published, the general enquiry address or a formal letter through the department
  office may be the correct channel.
- **Send from an institutional account.** A request from a university address, ideally
  cc'ing the supervisor, is far more likely to be actioned than one from a personal
  account.
- **The §3.3.4 reference is genuine** — the NAQMP 2024–2030 does state the goal of open
  data archives for researchers — but keep the tone as a polite request, not an
  entitlement; the plan is a statement of intent, not a commitment to any individual.
- **Do not overstate the thesis.** It is undergraduate research; the letter above is
  written to reflect that honestly.
- This request concerns data the Department may choose to share. It is entirely separate
  from the Department's public real-time portal, whose application interface is not a
  public data feed and must not be accessed by automated means; this outreach is the
  correct, permission-based route to the underlying data.
