# Horizon comparison -- how hard is the task, really?

All numbers are the **zero-parameter persistence rule** on the test split, observed
labels only (`is_imputed_pm25 == False`). Persistence predicts that the category h hours
from now equals the current hour's. It has no parameters and never sees the training set.

Its score is the floor any learned model must clear to have demonstrated anything. If a
model lands near these numbers, it has learned to echo its PM2.5 input.

Every horizon uses the same raw table, the same nine features, and the same chronological
split boundaries -- only the label offset changes.

---

## Persistence degradation

| Horizon | Label unchanged | Accuracy | Macro-F1 | vs h=1 h | Very unhealthy F1 | Hazardous F1 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **1 h** | 80.50% | 0.8011 | 0.7933 | +0.0000 | 0.8280 | 0.8823 |
| **6 h** **(primary)** | 53.87% | 0.5356 | 0.5118 | -0.2814 | 0.5104 | 0.5897 |
| **12 h** | 42.43% | 0.4221 | 0.3994 | -0.3939 | 0.4076 | 0.4618 |
| **24 h** | 30.40% | 0.3036 | 0.2862 | -0.5071 | 0.2758 | 0.3651 |

### Reading this

At **h=1 h** the label is unchanged in 80.5% of samples.
Persistence scores 0.7933 macro-F1 for free, which is why the
learned scores at that horizon (`reports/baseline_metrics_h1.md`) were not evidence
of forecasting ability -- they were evidence that consecutive hours look alike.

At the primary **h=6 h**, persistence falls to
0.5118 (**0.2814 lower**), and the label is unchanged in
only 53.9% of samples. Hazardous F1 alone drops
0.2926. The echo strategy has stopped working, which is exactly what makes the
horizon change worth doing: **there is now room for a model to be better than trivial.**

By **h=24 h** persistence has given up 0.5071 macro-F1 against h=1.
The decline is monotone across every step (h1->h6 -0.2814, h6->h12 -0.1124, h12->h24 -0.1132), so there is **no** diurnal rebound at 24 h -- landing on the same time of day does not recover the lost predictability. Difficulty rises with distance, full stop.

## Learned models at the primary horizon

At h=6, persistence scores **0.5118**. The trained
baselines (observed-only macro-F1 on test):

| Model | Macro-F1 | vs persistence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| RandomForest | 0.5173 | +0.0055 |
| XGBoost | 0.4991 | -0.0127 |

**The horizon change did its job, but the tabular baselines still do not clear persistence by a meaningful margin** -- the best gain is +0.0055. Two separate facts, and both matter: h=6 is a real forecasting task (persistence fell 0.2814 from h=1), and a model that sees only the current hour's nine channels still cannot exploit it. That is an *information* limit, not a model limit -- the tabular row is one timestep, while the sequence model gets all 24 hours of the window. Opening a real gap over 0.5118 is now the thing Phase 4 onward has to demonstrate, and it is no longer a foregone conclusion.

---

## What this settles

- **h=1 h was not a forecasting task.** It was a persistence task with a model
  attached. Any Phase 3 result quoted at h=1 should be read as such.
- **h=6 h is the primary dataset** for CTGAN, the sequence model, SHAP and the
  LLM advisory. `configs/default.yaml` sets `preprocessing.horizon: 6`.
- **h=1/12/24 h are kept for this figure only.**
  They are generated by the same pipeline into `data/processed/h<N>/`, and they are not
  training sets.
- Report the persistence floor next to every future headline number. A macro-F1 with no
  floor beside it does not say whether the model is working.

Regenerate with `python -m src.models.baseline --horizon-sweep`.
